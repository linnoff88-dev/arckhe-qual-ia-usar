Which AI to useGEMINI - GPT - CLAUDE

The cheapest AI on the price list may cost you more in the end.

You choose an AI by its price list. It gets something wrong, you ask again, and each attempt costs you: money if you pay as you go, or your quota if you pay for a subscription. Here you can compare AIs from Google, Anthropic, and OpenAI by the cost per successful task: what you spend before you get the task done.

Who wins in each tier Test with your task
In one sentence

This project compares ten AI models from three companies: Google's Gemini 4 (Neon, Argon, and Hydrogen), Anthropic's Claude (Fable, Opus, Sonnet, and Haiku), and the OpenAI models used by Codex (Astra, Sol, and Luna). It brings together official price lists and the Agent Arena leaderboard, a public leaderboard that measures AI agents (AIs that carry out tasks on their own, step by step) doing real work, checked on Nov 19, 2026. It's for people who pay for AI and want to choose without wasting money. You don't need anything to read it. To test with your own task, all you need are the apps you already use (ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini), or the testing kit, which runs the same task across all the AIs and tells you which got more right while spending less.

What the numbers show

  • All 10 got it right: for a simple task, the cheapest one wins.
  • Claude and GPT took 4 to 11 s; Gemini took 12 to 16 s.
  • Neon's cost (US$ 0.036) was higher even than Fable's (0.016) and far above Luna's (0.0001).
  • Hydrogen via API at standard effort cost about 2.5 times less than via subscription at high effort.

Why, and what remains to be measured

  • The main reason is effort: at high effort, Gemini thinks a lot before answering (2,000 to 3,000 tokens, compared with 100 to 800 for the others). Claude and GPT ran at standard effort, so the cost comparison isn't balanced.
  • Neon as a critic wasn't tested here: the test measured task execution, not review.
  • Next step for a fair comparison: run Gemini at medium and low effort too, and use a harder task that some models get wrong.

In practice

"How Nei chooses"

The numbers tell you what things cost. The experience of using them tells you what's worth it. These are Nei's day-to-day choices.

🪶 Luna and Haiku: affordable for simple, long tasks

GPT-6 Luna and Claude Haiku 5.5 cost pennies and got the simple task in our test right. They're good options for simple, long-running tasks: many repeated steps, high volume, work that doesn't require deep reasoning.

⭐ Terra and Sonnet: Nei's preference

In Nei's tests, the favorites are GPT-6.1 Terra and Claude Sonnet 5.5: they cost more than Luna and Haiku, but deliver good results more consistently. Within OpenAI, the order from least to most expensive is Luna, Terra, Sol, and Astra. Terra isn't in the tables above.

🔍 Neon as the critic

Nei currently uses Claude and Codex to review each other's work: models from different companies make different kinds of mistakes. Gemini 4 Neon could be that second opinion, the AI that checks and critiques, for someone who only has Claude or only has Codex. See how to enable Gemini Pro.

Gemini Pro in Claude and Codex

Use your Gemini subscription inside Claude Code or Codex

You pay for Google AI Pro and want Gemini to work alongside Claude or Codex, for example as an AI that checks and critiques? You can, but the way to do it changed in 2026.

⚠️ What changed: Gemini CLI has been retired

Gemini CLI used to be the way to use a Gemini subscription in the terminal. On June 18, 2026, Google stopped accepting Pro or Ultra subscriber accounts in it. Anyone who tries to sign in today gets the message: "This client is no longer supported for Gemini Code Assist for individuals".

The replacement is Antigravity CLI, Google's own Antigravity terminal program. It uses your subscription quota.

🔑 Why an API key won't work

A Gemini subscription doesn't cover Google AI Studio API keys. An API key has its own quota: on the free plan, Hydrogen responds, but Neon and Argon return "quota exceeded." To use them, you need a key linked to a credit card and pay as you go.

  1. We tried the API keys we already had.
    They were all on the free plan: Hydrogen ran, while Neon and Argon returned "quota exceeded."
  2. We tried Gemini CLI with a subscription account.
    Login worked, but Google refused access and told us to migrate to Antigravity.
  3. We installed Antigravity CLI and signed in with a Google account.
    It shows a link; you sign in with the account that has the Gemini subscription, copy the code that appears, and paste it back. The list included Gemini 4 Neon, Argon, and Hydrogen at several effort levels, as well as Claude Opus and Sonnet.
  4. Gemini started running through the subscription, called from inside Claude Code.
    The testing kit ran all three Gemini models this way, and all three got the receipt task right.

Ask your AI to set up Gemini

How to get started

Find out which AI costs less for your task

The leaderboard is a good starting point, but the right answer comes from your task. There are two options: do it yourself with apps you already subscribe to, or have your AI set up the test for you.

✋ Option 1: do it yourself with subscriptions you already have

You don't need to install anything or create a new account. Don't subscribe to all three? They have limited free versions you can use for the test.

  1. Choose a real task from your work and decide in advance what "done right" looks like.
  2. Ask each app to do the same task 3 times, with a subscription or free version (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini), always in a new conversation.
  3. Write down how many times out of 3 it was right without you correcting it.
  4. In the calculator above, enter the minutes you spend on each attempt (asking, reading, and checking) under "cost of each attempt," plus the attempts and correct answers you recorded. The lowest result uses the least of your time and quota.

🤖 Option 2: the testing kit runs your task across every AI you enable

You describe the task and what "done right" looks like. The kit sends the same task to each AI several times, an evaluator checks each response, and you get a report: how many were right, how long it took, and what each successful task cost. Your AI sets up and runs the kit.

1. Open your AI

Claude Code (included with a Claude subscription) or Codex (included with ChatGPT): these are versions of these AIs that work with files on your computer. Never used them? Stick with Option 1.

2. Paste the prompt below

It tells the AI to read the testing kit, format your task for it, and show you the plan without running anything yet.

3. Approve the test

Claude and GPT run through a Claude Code or Codex subscription. Gemini runs through a Google AI Pro subscription with Antigravity CLI (see how to enable it). Outside subscriptions, you'll need a pay-as-you-go account (the API) with a credit card. You approve which models to include, and it returns the report.

Read the "Which AI to use" project at https://github.com/inematds/qual-ia-usar (the README explains the testing kit in kit/testar.py).
I want to find out which AI has the lowest cost per successful task for one of my tasks: [describe the task and what "done right" looks like].
1. Tell me in plain English which 3 or 4 models are worth testing for this task and why.
2. Propose a small test: how many times to run each model and how much it should cost.
3. Don't call any models or spend anything before I say yes.
After I say yes, format the task for the kit, run the test, and show me the report.

The prompt tells the AI to show you the cost and wait for your approval before spending anything.

Common questions

Before you choose

Do I need to know how to code?

No. You don't need anything to read the comparison. To test, use Option 1 with the apps alone. Option 2 requires Claude Code or Codex and, outside a subscription, an account with a credit card at each company.

I pay a monthly subscription, not per use. Does this still apply?

Token prices apply to people who pay as they go directly to the company (the API). For subscribers, they're a guide to which model to use for each type of task. With a subscription, you don't pay per token, but the model that makes fewer mistakes also uses less of your quota.

So which AI is best?

For pure quality, Claude Fable 5.2 leads the leaderboard. For cost per task, Argon is the cheapest among models with a leaderboard score, until its price doubles on Dec 1. For your task, the answer comes from your test.

I already subscribe to ChatGPT. Is it worth subscribing to Claude or Gemini?

It depends on your task. Follow Option 1 with the free versions: the one that gets your task right most often is the one worth subscribing to.

How much does it cost?

The project and this guide are free. Testing the models costs whatever the companies charge, and the prompt tells the AI to show you the amount first.
